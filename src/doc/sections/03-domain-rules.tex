\section{Domain Rules}
\label{sec:domain-rules}

These are the rules the system enforces, in the order a driver meets them. They are what
the later sections implement, so they are stated here without discussion. Every number is
configuration; \cref{tab:params} gives the defaults and the shorter values used for the
demonstration. Nothing in the demonstration profile scales a clock.

\paragraph{Reservation and lease.} A reservation is for one connector at one station and
holds it for \texttt{LEASE\_SECONDS}, 15 minutes by default. When the lease runs out with
nobody plugged in, the connector frees itself: no operator, no client. Two minutes before
the end the driver is warned, when the lease is long enough for a warning to mean
anything. A reservation is only ever granted after the coordinator has
issued a \textbf{claim} for the vehicle (\cref{sec:p2}); the claim is what makes the
network-wide rule hold, one vehicle, one reservation, and it outlives the reservation by
\texttt{CLAIM\_GRACE\_SECONDS} so that it never expires underneath one that is still alive.

\paragraph{No-show and suspension.} Turning up resets the counter. After
\texttt{PENALTY\_NO\_SHOWS} consecutive expired reservations (two) the account loses the
right to reserve for \texttt{PENALTY\_DAYS} (one). The suspension is on the account, so a
second vehicle does not escape it, and it suspends reserving only: a suspended driver can
still walk in and charge on a free connector. It is enforced where the reservation already
passes, at claim time, by the coordinator. A missed reservation costs no money.

\paragraph{Authorisation.} A session starts when the charge point reports a cable, and
only then. On a held connector the vehicle has to be the one that reserved; any other is
refused and the reservation stays. On a free connector any registered vehicle starts a
walk-in session, with no claim involved. Identification is by vehicle: the account is
resolved from it on the station, which is why an isolated station cannot start a new
session (\cref{sec:station-failures}).

\paragraph{End of charge, grace, overstay.} A session ends when the driver stops it, when
the battery reaches 100\%, or when the coordinator revokes the claim. The cable is still in
the car at that point, and the connector stays occupied. For \texttt{OVERSTAY\_GRACE\_SECONDS}
(five minutes) that costs nothing; from the first second after it the connector is
\texttt{overstay} and every started minute is charged at the station's overstay rate. The
overstay is the one charge in the system that is a deterrent, and it is the only thing the
software can do about a parked car: no timer moves one, so the system notices, tells the
driver, and makes waiting expensive.

\paragraph{Power.} A site has a budget lower than the sum of its connectors' ratings. The
budget is split among the active sessions by \emph{max-min fair share with hand-back}
(\cref{sec:p5}): every session starts from an equal share, a car that cannot absorb its
share hands the surplus back, and the surplus is split again. The split is recomputed on
every arrival, every departure and every tick, and each charge point is told its limit. A
session whose share would fall below \texttt{MIN\_CHARGE\_KW} (6~kW) is \texttt{suspended}
instead: alive, drawing nothing, and told so.

\paragraph{Tariff and billing.} Each station has a tariff in cents per kWh and an overstay
rate in cents per minute. A session's cost is energy at the tariff plus the overstay
minutes, rounded up to the started minute; it is computed by the back office after the
session row has been written, on a periodic sweep, so billing is never a contended
resource. A driver reads the cost in the history page.

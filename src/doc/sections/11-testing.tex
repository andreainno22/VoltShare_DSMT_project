\section{Testing and Demonstration}
\label{sec:testing}

Three kinds of evidence: a unit suite that runs without the cluster, a load test against
the deployed containers, and a demonstration in which every scene maps to one problem of
\cref{sec:problems}.

\subsection{Automated tests}
\label{sec:tests-unit}

\begin{sloppypar}
The Erlang suite is 395 EUnit tests over the three applications, run by
\texttt{scripts/eunit\_check.sh}. Most of them exercise the station:
\texttt{vs\_connector\_tests} drives the state machine of \cref{fig:connector-states}
through every arc, including the reconciliation of a \texttt{plugged} that arrives after a
restart; \texttt{vs\_power\_tests} checks the fair share, the hand-back and the floor;
\texttt{vs\_driver\_proto\_tests} and \texttt{vs\_cp\_proto\_tests} check the two contracts
of \cref{sec:api} frame by frame; \texttt{vs\_claim\_client\_tests} covers the walk over the
coordinator list and the renewals. On the coordinator side
\texttt{vs\_coord\_membership\_tests} and \texttt{vs\_coord\_rebuild\_tests} cover the
quorum arithmetic and the rebuild, including the empty rebuild of \cref{sec:rebuild}.
\end{sloppypar}

None of these tests starts Cowboy, a coordinator or MySQL. The collaborators of every
module are injected as module names (\texttt{claim\_mod}, \texttt{db\_mod},
\texttt{conn\_mod}), and the suite substitutes stubs, \texttt{vs\_claim\_stub},
\texttt{vs\_db\_stub}, \texttt{vs\_cp\_stub}; the two protocol modules are pure functions
over a session map, which is why the WebSocket handlers hold no logic of their own
(\cref{sec:api-ws-driver}). Integration against a real coordinator uses
\texttt{vs\_mock\_coord}, a minimal coordinator that grants and refuses claims without an
election; it is a test tool outside the delivered system. On the Java side,
\texttt{BillingServiceTest} and \texttt{JwtUtilTest} are 14 unit tests on the pricing
arithmetic and the token; three integration tests need a database and a cluster.

\texttt{eunit\_check.sh} asserts the test count as well as the absence of failures. Three
ways of breaking the suite on purpose, a fixture that raises, an exit in a test process, a
generator that raises, all printed ``0 failures'' with 307, 299 and 286 tests: the total is
how far EUnit got in enumerating the tree, and a run can lose two dozen tests while
reporting success. The script therefore requires the summary to read exactly ``395 tests,
0 failures'', and updating the number is part of adding a test.

The count closes exactly on every row, $1 + (N-1)$, and the maximum grows roughly linearly
with $N$, which is the signature of the actor model of \cref{sec:p1}: $N$ requests for one
connector are $N$ messages in one mailbox, served in order. The same vehicle sent to five
connectors of two stations at once got one \texttt{ok} and four \texttt{NO\_CLAIM} in 5~ms,
which is \cref{sec:p2} seen from the client. A sustained run, ten drivers cycling
\texttt{join}, \texttt{reserve}, \texttt{cancel} on both stations for 150~s while two
charge points delivered 150 and 50~kW, produced 5620 requests, 2904 accepted, 2716 refused,
a maximum response of 62~ms, zero connectors left \texttt{held} and zero error reports on
any node. The limit of Cowboy and Ranch was not reached.

\subsection{The demonstration}
\label{sec:tests-demo}

One run of eight to ten minutes. Emulated charge points charge in the background on
\texttt{station2}, the presenter drives a browser and the failure commands, and a second
person starts the world and pastes the commands that answer the presenter's actions. The
demonstration profile shortens the leases so that a no-show matures in ninety seconds; no
clock is scaled. \Cref{tab:demo} is the map from what appears on screen to the problem it
demonstrates, with the date each scene was first seen working, because nothing is shown for
the first time in front of the examiner.

P6 and P7 have no scene of their own: they hold by construction across the whole run, and
a lost frame never desynchronises a page. The crash and the partition of a station are shown
one after the other on purpose, because they are two failures with two detectors and two
outcomes, and the difference is the point of \cref{sec:station-failures}.

\paragraph{What the demonstration found.} Rehearsing the whole run on 3 September, with
the suite green at 386 tests, turned up four defects that no test had seen. The
coordinator logged twenty-three things and not the one act it exists for, granting or
refusing a claim, so the central line of the presentation had nothing in the logs to back
it; two \texttt{logger:notice} calls fixed that. Four log panes, one per service, broke one
story into pieces, and were replaced by a single time-ordered stream of all seven
containers. The other two were in the emulated hardware: the world script did not restart a
charge point that died, and \texttt{cp.js} exited when its car unplugged, so a charge point
lived as long as one session. Unit tests protect the contracts; only running the system the
way it will be shown protects the demonstration.

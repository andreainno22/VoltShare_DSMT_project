\section{Future Works}
\label{sec:future}

Four extensions were identified while the system was being built. None of them is started,
and each is listed with the reason it was set aside.

\paragraph{Site power shared across stations.} Today the budget of \cref{sec:p5} is
coordinated inside one station. Two stations on the same grid connection would share one
budget, which is a divisible quantity to be lent and returned between nodes: a lease over a
continuous value, where the leases of this document are over exclusive resources. It is the
natural next problem for the coordinator, and it was left out because it would have added a
second network-wide invariant before the first one was demonstrably safe under failover.

\paragraph{Partitioning the vehicle space across coordinators.} Each claim would be
decided by the node responsible for a hash of the vehicle identifier, so no node would see
all the traffic and a failure would block one shard. \Cref{sec:p2} explains why it was rejected for now: every node must
agree on the partition map, the map must be rebalanced when membership changes, and each
shard still needs the election and quorum this project already has. The extension composes
with the current design instead of replacing it, and that is the order to build it in.

\paragraph{The waiting list.} Specified in \cref{sec:functional} and absent from the
delivered system. It needs a per-station queue with an owner, an offer that is itself a
lease (the same mechanism that stops a no-show from freezing a connector would stop an
absent waiter from freezing the queue), and a seventh notification kind that the driver
channel already names. The protocol side is in place: \texttt{join\_waitlist} is answered
as an unknown action until a process exists to answer it.

\paragraph{Native push notifications, and an operator console.} The overstay notice reaches
a driver who is looking at the page or who reopens the application later; there is no
mobile push. The grace period therefore assumes an attentive driver, which is a limitation
of the client channel and of nothing in the coordination logic. On the operator side the
back office is read-only: taking a connector out of service by hand, or pricing by demand,
would be commands from the web tier into the cluster, a direction the JInterface bridge
already carries for suspensions.

What is deliberately not on this list is a replicated log between coordinators. The rebuild
of \cref{sec:rebuild} costs a few seconds of refused reservations after a failover (a new
leader was serving 2.29~s after a partition of the old one, \cref{sec:detectors}); a log
would remove that window at the price of a replication protocol, which is a larger system to
get right than the one that was asked for.
